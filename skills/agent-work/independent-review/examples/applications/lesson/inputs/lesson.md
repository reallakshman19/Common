# Constant acceleration: a short learning task

Aim: a learner who knows signed velocity should distinguish direction from change
in velocity for one-dimensional motion with constant acceleration. This is a short
worked task, not a complete mechanics curriculum or a trial with actual learners.

Take right as positive. A cart starts at +3 m/s with constant acceleration -2 m/s²
for 4 seconds. Its velocity is v(t) = 3 - 2t m/s. At t = 4 s, v = -5 m/s: it is
moving left. It stops instantaneously at t = 1.5 s and then reverses direction.
Its signed displacement is 3(4) + (-2)(4²)/2 = -4 m. Distance is different:
the first part is 2.25 m to the right, the second 6.25 m to the left, totaling
8.5 m. An instant of zero velocity does not mean zero acceleration.

Check: if the same initial velocity instead has zero acceleration for 4 seconds,
velocity stays +3 m/s, displacement and distance are both 12 m, and no reversal
occurs. Units and signs must be preserved in each calculation.
