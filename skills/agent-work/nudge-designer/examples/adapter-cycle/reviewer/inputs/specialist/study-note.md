# Velocity and acceleration at the top of a throw

An object is thrown upward at **18 m/s**. Take upward as positive, assume no air resistance, and use constant gravitational acceleration **a = −9 m/s²**.

**Velocity** tells you how fast position is changing and in which direction. **Acceleration** tells you how velocity is changing. Here, every second gravity makes the velocity 9 m/s more negative:

**v = 18 − 9t**, with t in seconds and v in m/s.

| Time after release | Velocity | Acceleration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 s | +9 m/s, upward | −9 m/s², downward |
| 2 s | 0 m/s | −9 m/s², downward |
| 3 s | −9 m/s, downward | −9 m/s², downward |

At **t = 2 s**, the object reaches its highest point: 18 − 9 × 2 = 0 m/s. Its velocity is zero at that instant because it is changing from upward motion to downward motion. Gravity still acts, so its acceleration remains −9 m/s². Just before the top, at 1.9 s, v = +0.9 m/s; just after, at 2.1 s, v = −0.9 m/s. Velocity keeps changing through the zero value.

Compare an object **held at rest**. Its velocity stays zero over time, so its acceleration is zero. The upward holding force balances gravity. The thrown object has no holding force during flight.

Both objects can have zero velocity at an instant, yet different accelerations. To decide acceleration, ask **how the velocity is changing**, not only what the velocity is now.

Explain to yourself: At the turning instant, which quantity is zero? What keeps the velocity changing? Why is the held object different?
