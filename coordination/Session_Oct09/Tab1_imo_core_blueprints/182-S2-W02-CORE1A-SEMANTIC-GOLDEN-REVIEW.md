## Independently checked mathematics (not merely same-renderer comparison)

For every positive integer n, `n(n+1)(n+2)` has an even factor among n,n+1. The three exhaustive n (mod 3) cases map to divisible factors respectively n, n+2, n+1. Hence the whole integer product has divisors 2 and 3; gcd(2,3)=1 implies divisor 6. This argument establishes **all** n, rather than a finite test. To validate the changed exit, four consecutive n,n+1,n+2,n+3 contain precisely one 0 (mod 4) factor and a distinct 2 (mod 4) factor, so at least three powers of 2 occur; they also contain a multiple of 3. Therefore gcd(8,3)=1 implies 24 divides the product for **all** n. As secondary, independent machine falsification checks all n=1..120 for both claims and the respective factor selection, all six modulo-6 classes and 24 modulo-24 classes: all PASS. This does **not** substitute for Owner/human academic validation.

The candidate golden's distinct mathematics also passes exact substitution: x=2 gives 8≠9, and x=7/3 gives 9 exactly in `3x+2=9`; numeric-maths equivalence is **not** asserted between different questions.
