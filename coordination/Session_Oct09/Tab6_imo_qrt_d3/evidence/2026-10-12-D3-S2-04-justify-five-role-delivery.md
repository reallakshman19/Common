## Mathematical proof, protected W and stage semantics

For P(n)=n(n+1)(n+2) with n positive, 3 divides P for all n. With gcd(3,4)=1, 12|P iff 4|P. Residues n mod4 0,2,3 supply a factor divisible by4 respectively at n,n+2,n+1, while residue1 leaves n,n+2 odd and sole even n+1≡2 mod4. Thus **12|P iff n mod4∈{0,2,3}**. n=1 yields6, refuting “every” but not proving the iff. Protected main W is learner choice of the stronger factor-four condition, complete proof of three success classes and one failing class, and both necessity and sufficiency, not a repeated universal-by-six D2 proof. The Core2A preattempt H1/H2 ask only proof-vs-counterexample and exhaustive repetition; the authored H3 identifies the modulus/failing case but is explicitly **AFTER_ATTEMPT** `core2_support_plan` and the actual Chromium test confirms it cannot be opened without a nonblank typed commitment.

Core1A preserves registered BP-CORE1A-CONSTRUCTION three sequential figure stages for parity, mod3, coprime6 **not main modulo-four winners** and changes independent exit to false two-factor universal-by-six, testing why a factor3 is not guaranteed. Core1B tests false `6⇒12` argument through a separate written counterexample/warrant and independent boundary: four factors always provide a multiple of4 and of3, hence 12, unlike three factors. CORE2B genuinely changes `12` to `24` on the **same triple**, not mere text substitution: because factor3 is always present, eight is now required; for even n the two even factors contain at least 2³, while for odd n the only even is n+1 so success iff n+1 multiple8. Therefore **24|P iff n even or n mod8=7**. n=3 gives60, divisible12 but not24; n=7 gives504 divisible24. Both formulas tested over n=1..10000, 0 mismatches.
